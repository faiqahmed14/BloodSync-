Two ways to find blood

Not every request is the same, so we don't treat them the same.

Pakistan's blood supply runs mostly on the replacement system. BloodSync is built around that reality, plus a separate path for when the group is rare or the bank is simply empty.

Replacement match

For planned, routine needs

Hospitals ask a patient's family to arrange a replacement donor before releasing a unit. Any healthy donor of a compatible group usually qualifies. BloodSync finds the nearest willing donor immediately, no group rarity involved.

  • Matched purely by distance and availability
  • Typical use: surgeries, planned transfusions, thalassemia support
  • Fastest path, often resolved in minutes
Direct-need match

For rare groups or empty stock

Sometimes the exact group isn't sitting on a shelf anywhere nearby: an AB-negative unit, or a bank that's simply run dry. BloodSync widens the search across its full donor network to find someone with that specific group, wherever they are.

  • Matched strictly by blood group compatibility
  • Typical use: rare groups (AB-, O-, B-), depleted bank stock
  • Searches beyond your immediate city if needed

About BloodSync

Blood shouldn't depend on who you happen to know.

In Pakistan, finding blood in an emergency usually means the same scramble: a WhatsApp message forwarded to forty groups, a Facebook post asking strangers to share, a family member calling every contact they have who might be A-negative. It works, eventually, but "eventually" is a dangerous word when someone is on a hospital bed.

BloodSync exists to close that gap. It's a single, always-on network of registered donors across Pakistan, matched to requests instantly by location and blood group, built around how blood donation actually works here, not an imported model that assumes a system we don't have.

That means respecting the replacement donor culture most hospitals rely on, while still giving rare-group and empty-stock cases a dedicated, wider search, because a routine request and a genuine emergency don't deserve the same treatment.

What's next

A donor gets more than a thank-you.

Giving blood costs a donor real time and a bit of their day. BloodSync's roadmap is about paying that back, and making the system around donation stronger, not just the matching inside it.

Bank & hospital partnerships

A dashboard for every partner

Blood banks and hospitals we partner with get their own dashboard to manage requests, donor call-outs, and stock, directly from BloodSync, instead of running it over phone calls and registers.

Partner labs

Every donor is offered a free basic health checkup from a partner lab after donating, a way of looking after the people who look after everyone else.

Partner pharmacies

Discounts that say thank you

Donors get discount coupons at partner pharmacies after every donation, a small, practical way to appreciate the people keeping the network alive.

Myths and facts

Will donating make me weak?+

Most people feel fine. Your body replaces the fluid within a day and the red cells within a few weeks. Drink plenty of water, eat well, and skip heavy exercise for the rest of the day.

Does it hurt?+

You feel a quick pinch when the needle goes in. The donation itself, about ten minutes, is usually painless.

Can I donate if I am vegetarian?+

Yes. Staff check your haemoglobin before every donation. Iron-rich foods such as lentils, spinach and dates help you stay ready.

How long does stored blood last?+

Red cells can be stored for up to 42 days, but platelets only last about 5 to 7 days. That is why regular donors matter so much.

How many people can one donation help?+

A single donation can be separated into red cells, plasma and platelets, so it can help up to three patients.

What to expect

Your donation, step by step.

Quick screening

A short health questionnaire, plus a check of your pulse, blood pressure and haemoglobin. About 10 minutes.

The donation

You sit back while about 450 ml of blood is collected. It takes around 10 minutes.

Rest and refresh

Stay for 10 to 15 minutes with a drink and a snack before heading out.

It goes to work

Your blood is tested, separated and sent to patients who need it. Your 3-month rest period starts.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Medical staff always make the final decision on the day.
